READING TOOLS

A proposed diagnostic lens and research agenda. The companion audit records evidence and verification limits.

The paper has undergone a fresh source audit, a clause-level review, and post-correction cross-review. The second audit revised 34 review units and added one methodological reference. The resulting paper has 34 references. Its central contribution remains a proposed diagnostic lens and research agenda; no interviews, local outcomes, ethics approval, or empirical validation are claimed.

What the audit establishes

#

The checks support the revised paper's representation of the evidence available to this review. They found and corrected an overgeneralization about measurement denominators, incomplete or imprecise dates and citation pinpoints, several categorical interpretations, and wording that implied roles or outcomes not established by the sources. No fabricated source was identified. This is an internal research audit, not external journal peer review, a legal opinion, a compliance inspection, community validation, or an ethics determination.

How the three stages were performed

#

Source identity and primary evidence

Each cited work was checked against its primary publication, official record, or original organizational statement. Relevant passages, dates, authorship, identifiers, jurisdiction, draft status, and source type were examined. Direct-access failures were recorded. A primary search index or abstract was not treated as a complete fresh full-text read.

Claim and interpretation review

Six separate review assignments covered all substantive body paragraphs and all table rows. Statements were classified as documented facts, interpretations, proposals, normative premises, methods, questions, or evidence limits. Compound units were read clause by clause. Reviewers checked whether a source entailed the claim and whether the interpretation exceeded its population, time, geography, purpose, or study design.

Corrections and cross-review

All 34 amended units were cross-reviewed by a reviewer who did not originate the relevant recommendations, followed by a final reconciliation. That pass clarified two statutory references and restored an antecedent in a proposed-constraints sentence. Bibliography matching, coverage, source access records, document identity, and rendered pages were checked. These stages do not mean every underlying dataset or every reference inside each source was independently reanalysed.

Coverage and classifications

#

The detailed register contains 391 substantive review units spanning all body prose and table rows. Units are sentences, compound statements, or table rows, not 391 independent empirical claims. A sentence-indexing fragment containing only a citation continuation was merged with its preceding unit; no substantive content was dropped. The labels and coverage were manually reconciled after the initial indexing step. Titles, dates, captions, bibliography, and document structure were checked separately.

Limits that remain

#

Fresh direct full-text retrieval remained restricted for the GDPR, Jennings et al., and Paolo et al.; official or publisher-indexed relevant passages and bibliographic records were checked and the corresponding uses are narrow. The PIIDPA discussion cites the official guidance actually read; it does not imply direct reinspection of the entire statute. Malterud et al. is used only for the sample-planning principle supported by its primary abstract and metadata. These access limits should not be described as exhaustive full-text verification.

The paper's desk-review and subsequent empirical research requirements should be confirmed with MEW and the relevant institution before publication or the relevant research activities. No approval or exemption determination was obtained in this audit. The public nature of a document does not itself settle that status. Community preferences, private agreements, operational performance, net benefit, and framework validity remain empirical questions.

The search is targeted rather than exhaustive. It cannot establish that unlocated research or agreements do not exist. Legal or project-specific implementation requires the relevant current instruments and appropriate advice. A source check cannot turn a design proposal into a demonstrated outcome or guarantee that the paper contains no remaining error.
